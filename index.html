About

I am an AI researcher and PhD candidate at RWTH Aachen University, working on the development and evaluation of AI systems for real-world applications.

I design and implement end-to-end ML pipelines, from physiological time-series modeling and signal processing to multimodal systems that integrate speech recognition, computer vision, and large language models.

My recent work includes wearable ECG-based seizure detection, reproducible LLM-based evaluation of clinical simulations, and ongoing research on hospital admission, length-of-stay, and emergency department crowding prediction.

I am particularly interested in developing and evaluating trustworthy AI systems that bridge rigorous experimentation and real-world applications.

Selected Projects

HaMoJo: LLM and Computer Vision System for Structured Feedback

Large language models, computer vision, speech recognition

  • Developed an end-to-end research pipeline that integrates speech recognition, speaker diarization, computer vision, and locally hosted LLMs to generate structured feedback from recordings of medical students' simulation training. The system includes rubric-based scoring, programmatic validation, and reproducibility controls.
  • Pipeline: video/audio → transcription and diarization → behavioral features → LLM evaluation → structured reports.

System architecture, multimodal pipeline implementation, LLM evaluation methodology, and reproducibility engineering.

Links: GitHub repository, related publication

MoniPy: Deep Learning for Reliable Seizure Detection Using Wearable ECG

Deep learning, time-series modeling, model validation

  • Developed a wearable ECG seizure-detection pipeline combining physiological signal processing, automated data-quality assessment, and deep learning. Evaluated using subject-independent validation in a multicenter clinical study, with an emphasis on signal quality, model reliability, and generalization across patients.

My contribution: data processing, algorithm design, implementation, and evaluation; first author of the study.

Links: GitHub repository, published paper

FORECAST: Foundation Models for Hospital Patient Flow Prediction

Foundation models, temporal prediction, resource planning

Ongoing research
  • Applying foundation models to routine emergency department data to predict inpatient ward admission and subsequent length of stay, alongside research into emergency department crowding prediction.

My contribution: study design, model development, and evaluation.
